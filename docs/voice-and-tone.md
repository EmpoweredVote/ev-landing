# Empowered Vote — Voice & Tone

*Internal working doc · v2 draft · part of our design system*

How we sound when we write — in the newsletter, the app, on the site, in replies.
The examples are pulled from our own founder talks and homepage, so this is a mirror
of how we already sound at our best, written down so we can do it on purpose.

**The one rule:** *Voice* is our personality — it never changes. *Tone* is how we
dial that personality to fit the moment (a launch note vs. an error message vs. an
apology).

---

## 1 · Who we're talking to

We write with a **college-age reader as our lens**. Our primary audience is voters who want to be engaged in civics, but find it hard to find the time. They are smart, busy, and don't want to hear political spin, jargon, or being talked down to. They just want the facts to help them make more informed decisions.

The majority of our audience are probably not college-age, but that helps us set a bar for how we talk to them (and I don't mean in a hip Gen-Z/Alpha sort of way). We talk to the reader as a **peer** — a fellow citizen — never a "user," a "target," or other terms that dehumanize.

---

## 2 · Our voice in one line

> **A sincere friend who's done the homework — clear, warm, honest about what we don't know, and just glad you're here.**

In case that's not clear (it probably isn't), then read on, because we've got more description below.

---

## 3 · Personality — where we sit on the dials

We're **sincere and warm, with a hint of sparking joy** — but never meme-y, and never so stiff we sound like lawyers. Here's the calibration:

| Dial | We lean… | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| Serious ↔ Funny | **serious, not solemn** | We take the mission seriously; the joviality lives in the *details and the margins* — a stick figure playing fetch — not in the copy about democracy or money. |
| Formal ↔ Casual | **casual** | Plain and friendly. A person, not an institution. Contractions, "you" and "we." |
| Irreverent ↔ Respectful | **respectful** | Warm and kind. No snark, no dunking, no edgelord, no punching down. |
| Hype ↔ Matter-of-fact | **matter-of-fact, with warmth** | Earnest and understated. Real enthusiasm, but no exclamation-mark marketing. |

**The joviality test:** a moment should make someone *smile*, not laugh at a joke —
and it never comes at the expense of clarity, sincerity, or someone's dignity. When
in doubt, put the delight in the visuals and interactions (the bobbits), and keep the
words sincere.

## 4 · The four traits

The core of the doc. Each trait: what it means · **how to do it** · what it **sounds
like** (a real line of ours) · what's **not us**.

### Sincerity — we mean it, no hype
We say true things plainly and lead with value, not urgency.

- **How to do it:** cut hype words; lead with what's useful before any ask; say the real number, not the impressive-sounding one.
- ✅ *"I want to give my sincere thanks… the majority of our funding comes from micro-donations of around $5 to $10 a month."*
- ❌ *"Don't miss this GAME-CHANGING chance to revolutionize democracy!"*

### Empathy — meet people where they are
We let people decide for themselves and never pass judgment. We assume good faith and
leave the door open.

- **How to do it:** give the reader an out ("if not, that's okay"); write to where they're standing, not where you wish they were; never imply they're behind or wrong for asking.
- ✅ *"it allows everyone to decide for themselves if they're a fit… without ever needing someone to pass judgment."*
- ✅ *"if you're not able to donate, that's okay, too."*
- ❌ *"If you actually cared about democracy, you'd sign up."*

### Humility — small, early, honest about it
We'd rather undersell and over-deliver. We name our limits before anyone else has to.

- **How to do it:** state what's not done yet; credit the team and the people who help; describe scale honestly (eight volunteers, not "our team").
- ✅ *"empowered vote consists of eight volunteers."*
- ✅ *"it won't change the shape of what we build, but it will change the speed at which we get there."*
- ✅ *"We're not there yet."* (homepage)
- ❌ *"Empowered Vote is the definitive platform voters nationwide trust."*

### Candor — say the true thing, even the awkward part
We name the objection before the reader does, and we're transparent about money and
motives. Candor is what earns the trust the other three traits promise.

- **How to do it:** raise the obvious doubt yourself and answer it; be specific about how we make (and don't make) money; when something's rough, say so.
- ✅ *"at first glance, this target may appear idealistic… the type of thing that looks good on a bumper sticker."*
- ✅ *"Things will break. Data will be incomplete or wrong in places."* (homepage)
- ✅ *"it's not really a democracy if the path requires you to pay for power."*
- ❌ Burying the alpha caveats, or dodging "so how do you make money?"

## 5 · Three signature moves

The techniques that make the voice unmistakably *ours*.

1. **Explain a hard idea with a concrete story or analogy.** Reach for something
   lived and everyday *before* the abstract civic term.
   → *Mom's cake trick* (one kid cuts, the other chooses) for shared power; *"pay for
   power"* from video games; a *blind taste test* for Read & Rank.

2. **Coin a plain term, then define it in the same breath.** Naming a thing is
   powerful — but never leave a coined term undefined.
   → *"we call this lack of shared clarity around our facts **information pollution**."*

3. **Be explicitly, repeatedly non-partisan.** Show the idea before the identity.
   When politics could be read in, say plainly we're on nobody's side but the reader's.
   → *"Doesn't matter to us if you think of yourself as a liberal or a conservative. We
   don't care how much money you have… religion… nor the color of your skin."*

## 6 · How we treat the reader *(our Riot-heritage instincts)*

Three habits carried over from a "player experience first" world — translated to
"citizen experience first":

- **The reader is a peer, not a target.** We're building *with* people, not marketing
  *at* them. No dark patterns, no manufactured FOMO, no growth-hacking the copy.
- **We own our mistakes out loud.** When we get something wrong or break something, we
  say so plainly, fix it, and thank whoever flagged it. Transparency is the whole brand.
- **We leave room to smile.** Delight belongs in the margins — an animation, a warm
  aside, a well-timed bit of levity — never at the cost of clarity or someone's dignity.

## 7 · Tone by the reader's moment

Voice stays constant; tone flexes to what the reader is feeling right now.

| The reader is… | Dial toward… | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Curious / first-time** | Welcoming, plain, low-pressure | "Here's what this does, in one sentence." |
| **Confused / stuck** | Patient, concrete, no blame | "Not sure which race is which? Start with your ZIP." |
| **Celebrating a win** | Warm, a little jovial | "Your compass is set. Now the fun part." |
| **Hit an error** | Calm, honest, a way forward | "That broke on our end — this is alpha. Try again, or flag it and we'll fix it." |
| **Being asked to give** | Gentle, optional, value first | "If you're in a spot to chip in, it speeds us up. If not, that's okay — it all stays free." |
| **When we messed up** | Direct, accountable, human | "We got this wrong. Here's what happened and what we're doing about it." |

## 8 · Writing for everyone

This is our mission applied to words: we exist to **lower the cost of being informed**,
so the writing itself has to be easy for anyone to read.

**Plain language**
- Aim for about an **8th-grade reading level**. If a sharp 12-year-old couldn't follow it, simplify.
- **Short sentences** (aim under ~25 words). One idea per sentence.
- **Active voice, present tense.** "You choose your issues," not "issues are chosen by the user."
- **Everyday words:** *help* not *assistance*, *use* not *utilize*, *about* not *regarding*.
- **Define every civic term or coined word on the spot.** Assume no prior knowledge.
- **Make it scannable:** clear headers, short paragraphs, lists.
- **Read it aloud.** If you run out of breath or stumble, cut it down.

**Inclusive language** *(this is "idea before identity," applied to people)*
- Address the reader as **"you."** Use **"they/them"** when you don't know someone's pronouns — a name doesn't tell you.
- Mention someone's **identity** (party, religion, race, wealth, disability) **only when it's actually relevant.**
- **Don't assume** the reader's politics, income, or background. Everyone's welcome if they're looking for shared solutions.
- Avoid **ableist metaphors** ("crazy," "blind spot," "lame"), **gendered defaults** ("guys," "manpower"), and idioms that don't translate across cultures.

## 9 · Words we use / words we avoid

*A starter list — add to it as patterns show up.*

**Reach for:** citizen · shared solutions · "we're not there yet" · plain verbs ·
"you" and "we" · chip in · honest · alpha · everyone / folks

**Avoid:**
- **Hype:** revolutionary, game-changing, disrupt, unleash
- **Fake urgency:** act now, last chance, don't miss out
- **Talking down:** "simply just…", "obviously," "as everyone knows"
- **Partisan loaded language** and dog whistles of any stripe
- **Corporate filler:** leverage, synergy, best-in-class, solutions-oriented
- **"guys"** as a catch-all → *folks* / *everyone*

## 10 · Before you hit send

- [ ] Would this land for a 19-year-old **and** a 55-year-old donor?
- [ ] Could a sharp 12-year-old read it? (Reading level, sentence length.)
- [ ] Did I define any term I coined or any civic jargon?
- [ ] Where I'm explaining something abstract, is there a concrete example or story?
- [ ] Would someone on either side of the aisle feel this is fair to them?
- [ ] Am I honest about where we actually are — no overpromising?
- [ ] Is any ask gentle and optional, with value before the request?
- [ ] Did I keep it warm — is there a moment that makes someone smile, without undercutting the point?
- [ ] Can I cut hype words or filler?

## 11 · Before / after

**Newsletter subject line**
- ❌ 🚀 HUGE Empowered Vote update you can't miss!
- ✅ What we shipped in July (and what broke)

**The chip-in line**
- ❌ We need your support now — donate to help save democracy.
- ✅ If you're in a spot to chip in, even $5/month speeds us up. Not able to? Totally fine — everything stays free for everyone.

**Error / empty state**
- ❌ Error 500: request failed.
- ✅ Something broke on our end — this is alpha, so it happens. Try again, or flag it and we'll fix it.

**Explaining a feature**
- ❌ Read & Rank leverages a de-identified evaluation methodology to mitigate partisan bias.
- ✅ Read & Rank is a blind taste test for candidates: read what they actually said with the names hidden, rank what you like, *then* see who said it.
