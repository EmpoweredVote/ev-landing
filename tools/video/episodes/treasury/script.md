# Treasury Tracker — episode script

Inform pillar. Accent `#FFD426`, the same yellow as Empowered Compass.

Every claim below was checked against a component that renders in the shipped
app, not against a component that exists in the source. A card that is built but
never mounted is not a feature a viewer has. An earlier draft of this script had
a chapter on federal program origins — which bill created a program, who
sponsored it. The table, the loader and the card all exist, but nothing in the
client populates them, so it was cut.

Chapter 1 — the money leaves and nothing comes back

## L001
> Direction: a Bobit holds a pay stub. One line on it is picked out. `read`.
Look at a pay stub. Or a property tax bill, or a receipt with sales tax on it. There is a line on it where your money leaves you and becomes part of somebody's budget.

## L002
> Direction: the Bobit lowers the stub and looks up. `ponder`.
Almost nobody ever finds out what happened next. Not because it is a secret. Because the answer is long, and dull, and written for auditors.

## L003
> Direction: an enormous document drops in beside the Bobit and lands heavily.
Your city publishes its budget. But it is usually a few hundred pages, and it is full of lines that read: fund one-oh-one, department zero-eight, account four-two-zero-one-zero.

## L004
> Direction: the Bobit beside the document. `exhausted`.
Technically, you can read it. But 'Technically' is carrying a lot of weight in that sentence. Public and readable are not the same thing.

## L005
> Direction: title card on Inform yellow.
This is Treasury Tracker.

Chapter 2 — the budget as a picture

## L006
> Direction: `art` — the document collapses into one horizontal bar, divided into sized segments.
Treasury Tracker takes that document and draws it in a way anyone can read. The whole budget becomes one shape, and every department is a slice of it, sized by what it actually receives.

## L007
> Direction: `art` — the largest two segments are named.
Which means before you have read a single number, you already have the answer to the first question anybody asks. What is this government mostly doing?

## L008
> Direction: `art` — one segment is pressed and opens into a bar of its own.
Then you press a slice and it opens. The department breaks into its divisions. A division breaks into its programs. The same picture, one level down.

## L009
> Direction: `art` — a breadcrumb trail builds above the bar as it descends.
You can keep going until the slices stop dividing, and the trail at the top always tells you where in the budget you are standing.

## L010
> Direction: `art` — rows of line items, two money columns side by side.
At the bottom you reach the line items. What was budgeted, and next to it what was actually spent.

## L011
> Direction: `art` — a table of transactions: a name, an amount, a date.
And where the records support it, one step further. The individual payments. Who was paid, how much, and when.

## L012
> Direction: a Bobit `present` beside the finished bar.
That is the whole trick. It is not new data. It is the same data the government already published, arranged so a person can follow it instead of exploring spreadsheets.

Chapter 3 — more than one question

## L013
> Direction: `art` — two cards appear side by side, each named; a third joins them faintly.
A budget answers more than one question, so the tool keeps them apart.

## L014
> Direction: `art` — each card is picked out in turn as it is named.
Money out: how the money is spent. Money in: where it came from in the first place. And for the governments that publish it, a third: what the workforce is paid, department by department.

## L015
> Direction: `art` — the money-out bar alone, then the money-in bar joining it.
Most budget coverage only ever shows you the first one. Spending without revenue is half a story, and it is the half that makes everything look like waste.

Chapter 4 — across time

## L016
> Direction: `art` — a year is changed and every segment resizes.
Change the year, and the whole shape redraws.

## L017
> Direction: `art` — the same two segments across four years, one growing, one shrinking.
One year tells you what a government funds. Several years tell you how it is evolving. What grew, what shrank, and what quietly went away.

Chapter 5 — across levels

## L018
> Direction: `art` — the city bar shrinks and sits inside a county bar, then a state, then a federal.
And your city is not the only government taking your money.

## L019
> Direction: `art` — the four bars sit in a column, drawn identically.
You pay a city, a county, a state and a federal government, and each one keeps its own books. Treasury Tracker draws all four the same way, so you can step between them without learning a new chart each time.

## L020
> Direction: `art` — the federal bar, with a toggle above it.
The federal budget brings its own question. Do you want to see what the money is for, or who spends it?

## L021
> Direction: `art` — the same total redrawn twice, by purpose then by department.
Those are two completely different pictures of the same money. By purpose, and by department. You can switch between them and watch the shape change.

## L023
> Direction: `art` — an enormous figure shrinks into a small per-person figure.
Federal numbers can get pretty large, so you can switch the whole view out of raw dollars and into dollars per person. Trillions mean nothing. Your share of it means something.

Chapter 6 — where the numbers come from

## L024
> Direction: a Bobit `ponder` in front of the chart.
Which raises the question you should ask of any chart anybody ever shows you. Where did these numbers come from?

## L025
> Direction: `art` — a source line appears under the chart, naming a document and linking to it.
Out of the government's own filings. The published budget, the annual financial report, the Treasury's own spending records. Every view names its source and links to it.

## L026
> Direction: `art` — two descriptions side by side, one labelled as the government's wording.
Where a description is the government's own wording, it stays their wording, and it says so. Where we summarised one from the line items underneath it, that says so too. You can always get back to the original page.

## L027
> Direction: `art` — on the federal view, a panel opens with short headed sections.
On the federal budget the method is written out in full. What is counted and what is not. Why the picture can add up to more than the official total. Where a negative amount went. How far into the year the data runs.

## L028
> Direction: a Bobit `present` beside the open panel.
That panel exists because a chart with no method behind it is only a confident drawing. If you want to check our arithmetic, we tell you how.

Chapter 7 — what we actually cover

## L029
> Direction: a Bobit `standstill`. A map behind it, partly filled.
Treasury Tracker is in alpha.

## L030
> Direction: the federal layer fills completely; individual cities light up above it.
The federal budget is there for anybody. Below that it is city by city, county by county, state by state, and every one of them is a separate piece of work. Find the filing. Read it. Map it. Check it.

## L031
> Direction: the Bobit beside a plain message card. `offer`.
So search for your city. If it is not in yet, the site says so plainly, and it will not quietly hand you somebody else's budget instead.

Chapter 8 — what this is for

## L032
> Direction: a Bobit `standstill`, the budget bar behind it, calm.
It is worth saying what this is for.  

## L033
> Direction: a second Bobit `annoyed`, pointing at one small segment.
Not to make you angry about a line item. Angry is easy. 

## L034
> Direction: `art` — the single segment zooms back out until the whole bar is visible around it.
It's so you can vote with confidence and context.  Hearing a number on its own can be made to mean almost anything. That same number inside the whole picture usually means just one thing.

## L035
> Direction: two Bobits in front of the bar, `present` and `ponder`.
If we want a democracy that is run by the people, we need to make it easier for the people to understand where their money is going.
