# Empowered Essentials — episode script

Inform pillar. Accent `#FFD426`, the same yellow as Empowered Compass.

Every claim below was checked against a component that renders in the shipped
app, not against a component that exists in the source. An earlier draft had a
line about campaign money broken down by industry: `IndustryChart.jsx` is
written, and it is imported nowhere, so no viewer has ever seen it. It was cut.
A line about contact details went the same way — the profile does not carry
them. What the profile does carry instead is the seat's ballot status, which is
better material anyway.

Chapter 1 — the offices nobody can name

## L001
> Direction: a Bobit holds a ballot. The top of the column has names on it; the bottom half is a list of job titles. `read`.
Here are some jobs that are on your ballot. County assessor. Water district board. Community college trustee. Superior court judge. School board, seat four.

## L002
> Direction: the Bobit lowers the ballot. `confused`.
Most people can name the president, probably the governor, maybe one senator, and then it stops. That is not ignorance. Nobody ever handed you the list.

## L003
> Direction: loose sheets scatter across the screen, each one a different site, each a different shape.
The information exists. It is just kept in a dozen places. The city clerk has the council. The county has the assessor. The state has the legislature. The federal election commission has the money. None of them talk to each other.

## L004
> Direction: the Bobit stands in the middle of the scatter. `witsend`.
So to answer one plain question — who represents me — you would have to visit all of them, and already know what you were looking for.

## L005
> Direction: title card on Inform yellow.
This is Empowered Essentials.

Chapter 2 — one address, every office

## L006
> Direction: a Bobit `tap`, an address bar above it.
Essentials starts from one piece of information. Where you live.

## L007
> Direction: `art` — a point on a blank field, with boundary outlines growing around it one at a time, each labelled.
Your address sits inside a stack of boundaries. A city. A school district. A county. A state house district, a state senate district, a congressional district. They overlap, they are different shapes, and almost none of them line up with each other.

## L008
> Direction: `art` — the boundaries resolve into a list, grouped into bands: local, school, state, federal.
We take the address, work out every boundary that contains it, and hand back every office that comes with it. Grouped by level. Local at the top, federal at the bottom.

## L009
> Direction: `art` — during an election, candidate cards slide in beside the sitting officials.
And when there is an election running, the people asking for those seats appear next to the people already holding them.

## L010
> Direction: a Bobit `present` beside the finished list.
That list on its own is more than most voters have ever had in one place. But a name is only the start of a question.

Chapter 3 — the profile

## L011
> Direction: `art` — a profile card opens: portrait, office, term dates.
Open anybody on the list and you get their profile. Who they are, which seat they hold, and when that term runs out.

## L012
> Direction: `art` — a banner across the card reads that the seat is on the ballot, with a date under it.
If the seat is on the next ballot, the page says so, and it tells you whether the person holding it is running again or whether it is an open seat.

## L013
> Direction: `art` — a short ladder of named stances appears under the card, one of them marked.
Under that, where they stand. These are the same issues, on the same ladders, that the Empowered Compass uses. So a stance here and a stance there mean exactly the same thing.

## L014
> Direction: `art` — a small two-shape compass appears in the corner of the card, your shape under theirs.
If you have calibrated your own compass, theirs is drawn over yours right there on their page. Same spokes, same scale.

## L015
> Direction: `art` — the reasoning and a numbered source appear beneath a stance.
Every stance carries the reasoning behind it and the sources under that. Nothing on a profile is our opinion of a person. It is a position we can show our work for.

## L016
> Direction: `art` — one topic on the ladder stays empty while the others fill.
And where we do not have the evidence, we leave it empty. A blank is an honest answer. A guess is not.

Chapter 4 — who paid for the campaign

## L017
> Direction: `art` — a single horizontal bar, unlabelled, fills from the left.
Then there is the part people most want and least often get. Who paid for the campaign.

## L018
> Direction: `art` — the bar divides into four named bands.
Every dollar raised is sorted into four groups. Small grassroots donations. Large individual donors. Political action committees. And money the candidate loaned or gave to their own campaign.

## L019
> Direction: `art` — two bars of identical length, split in completely different proportions, side by side.
Those proportions tell you something a total never will. Two campaigns can raise the same amount of money and be funded by entirely different people.

## L020
> Direction: `art` — a list of named committees, then a search field over a donor list.
You can open the committees one by one and see what each of them gave. You can search the donors by name.

## L021
> Direction: `art` — a separate block appears beside the bar, labelled outside spending.
And kept separate from all of it, outside spending. Money spent for or against that candidate by groups the candidate does not control, which is a different thing and is worth not mixing in.

## L022
> Direction: a Bobit `showTo`, the source names appearing beside the chart.
All of it comes out of the public filings. Federal candidates from the Federal Election Commission, state and local from their own disclosure systems. We are not revealing anything. We are putting it where a person can actually read it.

Chapter 5 — the record

## L023
> Direction: `art` — a list of bills, each with a vote marked beside it.
For the officials who vote on things, we show the votes. The bill, what it did, and which way they went.

## L024
> Direction: `art` — a stance from chapter three and a vote from this one sit side by side.
What somebody says they believe and what they voted for are two different records. Essentials puts both on one page and lets you notice the distance yourself.

Chapter 6 — what we actually cover

## L025
> Direction: a Bobit `standstill`, plain and level. A map behind it, partly filled.
Now the honest part. Essentials is in alpha.

## L026
> Direction: the map fills completely, but for one layer only.
Type any address in the United States and you will get your members of Congress, and the people running for the House. That part works everywhere.

## L027
> Direction: individual areas on the map light up.
The full picture — every office from the city council up — lives in our alpha communities. Those are the places we have taken in, curated, and checked by hand.

## L028
> Direction: a smaller number of those areas light a second time, brighter.
And a smaller set of those again have stances on their local officials, because reading a council member's record takes longer than listing one.

## L029
> Direction: one more area lights up, slowly.
Both lists grow every week, and they grow slowly on purpose. Each one is a real roster, read out of a real clerk's office. Not a scrape and a hope.

## L030
> Direction: the Bobit beside a plain message card. `offer`.
If your area is not covered yet, the site says so. We would rather tell you what is missing than show you a thin answer and let you think it was the whole one.

Chapter 7 — why the bottom of the ballot

## L031
> Direction: `art` — the ballot from scene one returns, the lower half brighter than the top.
It is worth saying why the bottom of the ballot gets this much work.

## L032
> Direction: `art` — short labels appear around the lower half: zoning, the school day, the water bill, the assessment, the courtroom.
Those offices decide most of what you actually touch. Your zoning. Your school day. Your water bill. What your house is assessed at. Who hears your case.

## L033
> Direction: `art` — a handful of dots beside a local seat, an enormous field of dots beside a federal one.
And they are won with the fewest votes. A council seat can turn on a couple of hundred people. Which means the bottom of the ballot is exactly where one informed voter counts for the most.

## L034
> Direction: a Bobit `ponder` beside the ballot.
The reason people vote the top of the ballot and guess the rest is not apathy. It is that the rest was never made readable.
