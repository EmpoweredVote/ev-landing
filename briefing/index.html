The stance total went up. The number of positions a voter can read went down.

Both are true, and until this week nothing here could tell them apart. An audit read every stance whose own reasoning admits it has no evidence — “no documented position”, “scored based on her party”, “assigned a moderate value” — and withdrew the ones that failed. 1,629 positions now stand withdrawn, 1,374 of them in the last five days. A withdrawal is written as a blank in the newest season, which suppresses the old answer everywhere a voter could meet it — and a blank is a new row, so the stored total climbed from 36,438 to 38,311 while the thing the headline claims to count fell.

The figure this page has published for months was a row count, not a position count. The two had parted company before the audit even began: one person can hold a Season 1 and a Season 2 answer on the same topic, and both rows were counted. 38,311 rows resolve to 33,629 person-and-topic pairs, of which 32,000 carry a position a voter is served. That is the headline above: 6,311 below the stored total beside it, 4,438 below what we printed five days ago, and not comparable with any earlier edition. The refresh script now measures both and warns when they move in opposite directions.

Two things went right in the same five days. The thirty-seven proposed city positions the last edition left in front of a human being were all reviewed and published — the first time this programme has carried research through to a voter — and Minnesota moved up a tier on the map because of it. And a LegiScan refresh took the legislative record from 121,178 votes to 8,448,319 across 29 state legislatures plus Congress, which is the evidence base the audit above has spent a week insisting on. The detail for both is below.

On the Record

Speaker-attributed, searchable transcripts of public meetings, synced to the original video. What a blank asserts, and what it does not

Being exact about what a withdrawal claims matters. A blank asserts that the row’s own reasoning does not support the rung it carries. It does not assert that the person holds no view. The commonest defect was an absence sitting beside an affiliation — a caucus, a committee seat, a scorecard percentage, an endorsement, a district, an ideological label — used to place someone who had said nothing on the subject. Several rows stated the method outright: “absence of activity on this topic places her at…”, “insufficient evidence to differentiate from baseline”.

The first tier was read by hand, one row at a time. A detector swept all 38,405 rows carrying reasoning and produced 317 voter-facing candidates; 307 were actionable, and a person read every one. 206 failed and were withdrawn; 101 passed and were left exactly as they were. Rows were spared where the absence rules out a neighbouring rung on top of real evidence, where the omission sits inside the person’s own document, and where a score came with the record behind it. An audit that withdrew everything it looked at would not be an audit.

Nothing was added to the certified corpus this cycle. One stratum of one evidence class is still the only measured part of it — 57 state-level, record-based stances, certified October 3 at 0.830 and 0.932 with no severe disagreements. News-sourced and statement-sourced evidence are not certified, nor is the local tier. The audit above is the manual counterpart to that measurement, not a substitute for extending it.

Thirty-seven rows, four days, and the first closed loop

The question this programme has never been able to answer is whether anything it produces reaches a voter. Now it has. The six cities researched last cycle produced 1,645 rows, of which thirty-seven proposed an actual position; the rest were scope findings, proof that a city may not decide the thing at all. Every one of the thirty-seven was reviewed by a person and published: Duluth 18, Saint Paul 12, Charlotte 5, Miami 1, Bradenton 1. Tallahassee produced none that survived review.

The shape of the result is the finding. Minnesota was chosen third precisely because it reserves less to the state than North Carolina or Florida do, and it held: Duluth and Saint Paul produced 30 of the 37 between them, from 17 people; Charlotte 5, Florida 2. A city with the power to act leaves a record; a city without it leaves a citation to the statute that stopped it. Both are worth publishing, and only one shades a map.

Two Washington towns went from nothing to voter-facing inside the same five days — Redmond and Duvall: 16 city seats, 13 headshots, coverage chips and banners, budgets loaded from their own filings, and 12 published positions across 7 people. The constraint this page named last cycle was the review queue, and the queue was cleared. It will be the constraint again the moment the next tranche lands. That has not been solved; it has been paid off once.

Follow the money

The third leg, alongside votes and stances. The platform holds 29.1 million individual campaign-finance contributions totaling $8.6 billion, covering 2,250 federal politicians and candidates across 24 election cycles (1980 to 2026). Every record traces to its original FEC filing, and superseded lines from amended filings are retired individually rather than counted twice. Unchanged this cycle: the widened matching and the 33 disputed surname links were last cycle’s work, and nothing has arrived since.

Treasury is still unmeasured rather than low — but the half that matters moved again

An earlier edition published a rate here — seven people in a quarter, as 5% of 152 visitors — and withdrew it. It stays withdrawn. The events were sound; the denominator was not, because Treasury pushed its in-app navigation into the address bar without recording a page view, so every visitor was filed against the bare domain. The direction was wrong too: this quarter those few people produced 160 category drills, 100 entity selections, 100 line-item views and 56 year changes.

The two ways of counting still disagree by more than three times. 82 people reached a budget address; only 22 fired any past-the-front-page event. The split explains it: 59 landed on our own budget, one click from the donate page — a transparency visit, which selects nothing and so fires nothing. That leaves 23 on some other government’s budget, and that half has now risen twice running, 16 to 19 to 23, while the donate half went 47 to 55 to 59. The day the non-donate half outgrows the donate half is the day this section gets a real denominator. It is not that day.

The Road Ahead

How much further we have to go, honestly.

Now to Nov

Election readiness

Finish the audit. The first tier was read by hand and closed; the tail bands are still being worked, and 24 rungs are known to ask a different question in Season 2 than they did in Season 1, which means the next pass repairs forward rather than blanking. Then put the 8.4 million votes to work — the evidence class the certified stratum rests on is exactly the one that record feeds, and it now covers 29 legislatures instead of a handful. 3,861 legislators now carry at least one recorded vote, which is the population that record can speak for. Finish the 2026 candidate waves, land the trivia slot-C fix that did not move this cycle, write the one collection an audit cannot fix, and time the Treasury page properly. Success in November is one thing: any voter in a served area can look up every name on their ballot and follow the money behind it.
