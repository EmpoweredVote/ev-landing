Georgia twice over — and the largest load we have ever run arrived without its paperwork

This is a seven-day cycle, and Georgia is the shape of it. No edition published on Friday, so this one covers a week rather than the usual three or four days. 11,167 governments now have budget data, up 2,246 — and every one of those 2,246 is a government that had none before. Georgia was swept end to end: 440 cities, 152 counties and 103 towns, which is the state complete. New York was finished: 933 towns, 561 villages and 57 counties, on top of the school districts loaded last cycle. Line items went from 5.3 million to 10.4 million, a doubling for the second cycle in a row.

And not one of those 207,113 budgets carries a provenance grade. Every single one is filed as unknown. Last cycle this page praised the opposite: the 717 school districts all landed graded, and the pile of budgets whose provenance is simply unknown sat unmoved at 38,491. That pile is now 245,604. The old 38,491 did not move — the growth is this week’s load, to the row, and the school districts still carry their grade. We took on five times more ungraded provenance in a week than the platform had accumulated in its entire history. The governments are real and the figures are real; what is missing is the line saying where each number came from and whether anyone audited it. That line is most of what separates this platform from a spreadsheet, and this week it is what got dropped to move fast.

The stance line restarted, and it is smaller than it sounds. Last edition reported that nothing had been written to the stance table since October 9 and called restarting it the one job that mattered. 72 positions landed — at 06:01 this morning, in a single batch, covering 15 Georgia officials across 11 topics. The eleven days before it were empty. It is the right state and the right work, and it is one batch rather than a week of research — written, as it happens, in the same minute this page began gathering its numbers. Eleven days of nothing, ended an hour before publication, is not yet a restart.

What’s nextIt is not a code defect and it is still a tell. The reason sorted questions pile into C is measurable: on a numeric question the true value is one of the two middle options 188 times out of 211, 89.1%, where chance is 50%. The models writing these questions bracket the right answer, offering values above and below it, so sorting puts it in the middle. A player who learned “pick a middle number” would beat chance on that half of the bank — which is the thing the original fix existed to prevent. The open question is no longer whether the generator works but whether numeric options should be sorted at all. The repository’s own placement checks pass 47 of 47. What an unknown provenance grade actually costs

A provenance grade is the sentence that says where a budget figure came from and who, if anyone, checked it. Ours run from an audited statement prepared under government accounting standards, through a compilation drawn from audited figures, down to a government’s own unaudited filing — which is what a small town’s budget usually is, and is an honest answer. “Unknown” is not a grade on that scale. It means nobody recorded the answer. Someone reading one of these 2,246 new governments sees real figures with nothing behind them, and has no way to tell a town that published audited accounts from one that typed a number into a form.

Why it happened is not a mystery, and it is worth saying plainly: grading is per-source work, and the sweep was built for speed. The 717 school districts last cycle could be graded because they came from one publisher with one known practice — and this week they were reconciled against their own audited statements, which is the next step past a grade. Georgia’s municipalities and New York’s towns and villages arrive through forms whose provenance differs by the generation of the form, and one of this cycle’s commits exists precisely to read an older form generation. The work is identified, not done, and this page will report it the week it is. Until then the honest framing is that we hold these budgets and cannot yet tell you how good they are.

Follow the money

The third leg, alongside votes and stances. The platform holds 29.2 million individual campaign-finance contributions totaling $8.6 billion, covering 2,265 federal politicians and candidates across 24 election cycles (1980 to 2026). Every record traces to its original FEC filing, and superseded lines from amended filings are retired individually rather than counted twice. This cycle’s work was again subtraction: the California adjudication ratchet was banked at 437 confirmed independent-expenditure sources, down from 520, and the mechanism that had been quietly re-absorbing rows an editor already ruled on was closed. A money page that credits a contribution to the wrong person is worse than one that is missing it, which is why a figure going down counts as progress here.

The Road Ahead

How much further we have to go, honestly.

Now to Nov

Election readiness

Grade the budgets, and keep the stance work going past one morning. Those are this cycle’s two debts and they are the same debt in different data: a figure we hold but cannot yet stand behind, and a figure we stand behind but have barely added to. With two weeks to the election, the 207,113 ungraded budgets are the larger risk, because they are already in front of readers. The audit’s tail bands are untouched — the withdrawal count has not moved — and the rungs that changed between seasons still need a ruling before any answer carries across. Then put the 8.4 million votes to work — that record feeds exactly the evidence class the one certified stratum rests on, and nothing has been certified in three cycles. Finish the 2026 candidate waves, decide whether numeric trivia options should be sorted at all, write the one collection an audit cannot fix, and time the Treasury page properly. Success in November is one thing: any voter in a served area can look up every name on their ballot and follow the money behind it.

An interactive Purpose mind map walks the whole vision — the thesis, and the Why, How, Who and What — with a short explainer on every concept and pillar feature.

Sources and method. App statuses from git history across all repositories, read from each repository’s published branch rather than from a working copy, with an explicit time of day on every window; this cycle covers the seven days since the last edition was committed, because no edition published on the scheduled Friday. One repository, Fallacy Finders, has no remote, so it is the single exception and is read locally. Database counts queried live on October 20, 2026. Reach figures are from PostHog — the 30 and 90 days ending October 20, 2026, internal and test traffic excluded, each app counted once across every hostname that serves it; the substitution used to exclude that traffic was controlled against the native filter on four rows and agreed exactly. “Visitors” and the per-app action counts are both unique people over the same window, so a rate between them is fair but is not a strict funnel. The headline stance figure counts positions, not stored rows — the newest season’s answer per person and topic, where that answer is not a withdrawal — and is not comparable with editions before October 10; the coverage table reconciles against the stored count, which is printed beside it. The provenance figures are dated from the budget rows themselves, which carry a creation timestamp only for recent loads — 287,006 older budgets have none, so the unknown-grade pile is split into this cycle’s 207,113 and a pre-existing remainder of 38,491 rather than tracked as a time series. The trivia split between designed and defective answer placement was computed with the trivia repository’s own placement module, not a database query, over every question generated since the fix. A politician’s state resolves through a fixed order of jurisdiction links, and federal seats are identified structurally, which is why they form one row. This edition is permanently archived at empowered.vote/briefing/2026-10-20 and will not be edited after publication. In keeping with the platform’s anti-partisan commitment, nothing in this report is colored red or blue.
