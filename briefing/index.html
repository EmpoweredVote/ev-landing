What we can say when the honest answer is nothing

We can now tell a voter that we researched someone and found no position. Until this week our only honest answer was an empty space on a profile. On October 26 we recorded 102 such findings, all on one topic, and they are live. Each says that we researched this person and found no public stance on Deportation Priorities. We checked it from outside while writing this. The public API returns the finding, and the deployed Essentials bundle carries the card that shows it. The last edition said this code was written and gated behind an unapplied migration. The gate is open.

None of the 102 could be seated, and the reason is structural rather than lazy. All 102 hold a Season 1 answer on the old immigration topic, which has no pin in the open season. Nothing of theirs on the subject reaches a voter. So we researched them again on the successor topic. At state and local level that topic can only be seated by an official’s own words, and 85 of the 102 hold state or local seats. Its rungs separate on who is left alone, which is the clause officials never state. Pro-enforcement words fit three rungs at once, and protective words fit three others. We looked twice, seated nobody, and the page now says so instead of showing a blank.

The audit spent the cycle re-reading answers rather than adding any. It wrote 459 rows in the open season, and 456 landed on a pair that already held a Season 1 answer. 252 withdrew that answer and 204 replaced it. Positions a voter is served fell 31,842 to 31,593, while stored rows rose to 38,930. Not one politician gained a first position, and no map tile moved. The last edition described a withdrawal as mostly a rewrite of an existing row, at 240 rewrites to 44 inserts. This cycle it is the other way round, 226 inserts to 26 rewrites. Both readings are right for their own cycle, so we now measure creation and update dates together.

And the Treasury page has been timed, which pays a debt five editions old. We drove the live site with a browser, three times, each from a cold start. The search box appeared in 0.4 to 0.9 seconds. The 1.95 MB index of places arrived in 1.1 to 1.4 seconds. A city’s headline spending figure rendered in 2.1 to 2.6 seconds. That is one machine on one home connection rather than a laboratory. It is still the first real measurement anyone has taken. Doing it also turned up a correction we have owed since September 11, which is in the archive note below.

Platform Core & Infrastructure

The shared spine every app plugs into.

ev-accounts

Auth, tiers, XP and gems, politician database, TIGER geofencing, elections. 72 commits in four days, the busiest repository again. The new tool is a contradiction check. It finds rows that cite the same instrument, take the same side of it, and sit on different rungs. At most one of them can be right. Re-run for this report, it names 333 such groups covering 2,283 rows. Eight migrations adjudicated the largest of them, one instrument at a time. Elsewhere Maine’s municipal skeleton was finished, six mayor offices turned out to be council seats, and 16 vacancies were dated rather than left looking like people with no name. 2,277 federal politicians carry finance records.

The check cannot see the defect it most needs to find, and its own documentation says so. It reports disagreement, not error. A mapping that is wrong for everyone who cites it produces no disagreement at all. It can also compare only 28% of voter-facing rows, because a numeric bill needs a legislature to scope it and a descriptive phrase is not an instrument. A clean run would mean that no two rows disagree, never that the mappings are right.

One migration was reverted the next day because its premise did not hold. Four real display names had been replaced with pseudonyms, and the replacement was undone. No version number is printed on this card, because nothing in the repository produces one.

The provenance debt, a second edition on

A provenance grade is the sentence that says where a budget figure came from and who, if anyone, checked it. Ours run from an audited statement prepared under government accounting standards, through a compilation drawn from audited figures, down to a government’s own unaudited filing. That last one is what a small town’s budget usually is, and it is an honest answer. “Unknown” is not a grade on that scale. It means nobody recorded the answer.

We said last time that we would report this the week it was paid. It is not paid, and this is that report. Not one of the 245,604 moved. Last time we could at least point to a single row that had been touched, and this time it is zero. The four days went to the stance audit, to the research findings at the top of this report, and to a dependency and security sweep across ten repositories. Those were worth doing and this was not done. Repeating the figure unchanged is how a reader can tell a debt from an intention.

Follow the money

The third leg, alongside votes and stances. The platform holds 29.2 million individual campaign-finance contributions totaling $8.6 billion, covering 2,277 federal politicians and candidates across 24 election cycles (1980 to 2026). Every record traces to its original filing, and superseded lines from amended filings are retired individually rather than counted twice. Nine more politicians gained finance records and nothing else here moved. One filing match was disputed rather than deleted. The link is marked disputed instead, because deleting it would let the matcher write the same guess again. Only confirmed links are counted as money.
