A loud cycle for the map and a quiet one for the reader

We wrote 682 stance positions in three days and 670 of them went into a season no voter can open. The other twelve went into the open season. The research map now shades 343 officials it did not shade on Tuesday. Eight of those 343 have a position a reader can see. Georgia gained 116 officials, South Carolina 64, Kentucky 56, Missouri 44 and Wisconsin 42. All of that work is real. None of it has been published.

The readable total moved for the first time since October 29. It reads 31,552, up twelve. The twelve belong to six Santa Cruz city councilmembers and two Corte Madera town councilmembers, across three topics. We checked two of them from outside the database, and the live interface returned both of their answers. Twelve rows in fifteen days is the whole of what a reader has gained.

Draft Season 3 now holds 2,994 rows. It held 2,324 on Tuesday and 2,086 on the Friday before that. Gun policy accounts for 1,496 of them and voting rights for 1,448. We asked two editions ago whether to open the season or stop filling it. We asked again on Tuesday, and have since added 670 more rows to it.

The election half moved properly. 1,189 of the 2,343 races on our November ballots have a winner, against 1,093 on Tuesday, and they returned 1,235 winners. Two of those results are certified, which is the first time we have been able to write that sentence. Both are South Carolina congressional seats.

A link that opens is not the same as a link that proves anything

Last edition we described a new gate that asks the live web whether our citations still answer. This cycle it kept finding a different fault: links that answer perfectly and point at the wrong thing. Every California State Controller budget we hold carried one of four addresses. A reader who opened the source on a single city’s 2023 budget arrived at a grid of every city in California, for every year. Nothing was broken. Nothing was proved either.

24,354 of those budgets now cite their own entity and year, one distinct address each. The publisher runs a per-entity view, and the repair record says every row was checked against it before anything was written, with 24,352 of 24,354 tying to the dollar. That repair is most of why our distinct-citation count jumped from 1,857 to 26,386, and almost none of it is new documents.

The repair is not finished, and we only found that by counting. 8,622 budgets still carry one of the four statewide links, every one of them between 2017 and 2025. Those are the same years in which other rows now carry their own address. We do not know yet whether that second set was missed or deliberately left, and we would rather print the number than wait for the explanation.

The gate also had to stop believing a silent host. The Internet Archive rate-limits by host and, once tripped, stops answering rather than refusing out loud. A backoff written per URL cannot see that, because each citation discovers the limit on its own and deepens it for the next.
