Seven days, and most of them went to election results

We recorded 710 more winners from the November ballot. Five state legislatures account for nearly all of them, and a voter in any of those states can now look up the person who won their seat. The county tier opened too, which it had never done before.

The Compass moved much less. We wrote 1,655 positions and 1,635 of them went into the next season of research, which no voter can open yet. Twenty reached the site, for fourteen local officials. That is the whole of what a reader gained from a week of research.

Treasury Tracker closed its largest gap. Budget figures with no link to the document behind them fell to 298. Massachusetts is two thirds of the repair and California most of the rest, and the two were done to different standards.

Corrections

What this edition fixes in the one before it. A published edition is never edited; the next one says what it corrects.

We are withdrawing three Associated Press figures we printed on Friday. That edition said AP’s own state files hold 5,759 state legislative races and that AP had called 5,619 of them. It also said AP reported 319 as certified. We read all 51 of those files again for this edition, and every one answered. Between them they hold 644 races, and not one of them is a state legislative contest. What AP can settle is the US House. It has called 429 of its 435 House races. Fifty-six of those are certified.

We also said that two certified results were the first we had been able to write about. That is true of the November ballot and wrong about our records. Our records hold 491 certified results from this year, the earliest of them from an election in May. The two South Carolina congressional seats are still the only certified November results we hold.

The ballot basis changed again. Friday’s edition counted 2,343 races in the 53 elections dated November 3. The same elections now hold 2,303. Two records name 29 Oregon races that came off. Fifteen are state senate seats Oregon did not elect this year, and fourteen are duplicate rows for contests that elect several members on one line. We cannot account for the other eleven. Figures on either side of that change do not compare row for row.

The portrait licence gap widened. We reported 694 portraits with no usable rights statement, and it is 743 today. Portraits whose licence we record as unknown rose from 174 to 224, which is the whole of the change.

No edition went out on Tuesday. The job that writes this page wrote one and never published it, so empowered.vote/briefing has carried the November 13 edition all week. This edition covers the whole seven days, and every figure in it was measured again tonight.

Platform Core & Infrastructure

The shared spine every app plugs into.

ev-accounts

Auth, tiers, XP and gems, the politician database, geofencing and elections. 137 commits, more than every other repository put together, and most of them were election results. Massachusetts, Maine and Maryland gave us 556 winners between them, with Oregon and Nevada behind. The total from the November ballot is now 1,945.

The county tier is no longer empty. It held no winner on Friday and holds four today. Two are Multnomah County in Oregon, read from a ranked-choice results report. Two are Racine County in Wisconsin, read from the county clerk’s own summary. We hold 103 county races, so four is the honest shape of it.

The winners come from three kinds of source. 1,725 of them are Associated Press calls. Ballotpedia gives us 114 and election officials 106. Twenty-one carry no recorded timestamp at all, which is a gap in our bookkeeping rather than in the result.

Most of the rest of the week went to gun-policy research that reads a legislature’s own roll-call record. Fourteen states ran this cycle, among them Minnesota, Florida, Kansas and Ohio. None of it is public, because all of it lands in the next season.

The stored roll-call record did not move. It reads 11,557,651 legislative votes, the same figure as Friday and as the Tuesday before that. Recording a winner and recording how someone voted are different jobs, and only the first one happened again.

We re-ran the contradiction check for this edition. It reports 340 groups covering 2,347 rows. Friday’s run found 337 groups, and nothing has adjudicated any of them. It reads 35,320 rows a voter could meet and can key only 9,328. The check reports disagreement rather than error, and its own documentation says a mapping that is wrong for everyone who cites it produces no disagreement at all.

No version number is printed on this card, because nothing in the repository produces one.

Where the budget citation work got to

Budget figures carrying no address a reader could open fell to 298 this week, from a pile four publisher families had made. It is the largest single move we have recorded on this question. A separate fix mattered as much. A figure cited without a fetch date used to show no source link at all, and roughly 24,500 of them were in that state.

Massachusetts is the half we would show anyone. The state’s own reporting service answers per town, per year and per kind of figure. So the 16,829 rows we rewrote now carry 16,829 distinct addresses, and opening the source on one town’s accounts for one year lands on that town, that year.

California is the half that is not finished. 7,684 rows now cite one of seventeen statewide files, one per year, each shared by about 480 cities. A reader who opens that source downloads every city in the state for that year. The link answers. It does not show the row.

The four statewide dashboards have not moved either. 8,622 California budgets still cite one of them, every one dated between 2017 and 2025, exactly as on Friday. We said then that we did not know why those rows were left behind, and we still do not.

We found the California problem by counting distinct addresses rather than by checking that links resolve, and the same shape shows up elsewhere. A single New York address is cited by 274,428 of our rows.

Follow the money

The third leg, alongside votes and positions. The platform holds 29.6 million individual campaign-finance contributions totaling $8.9 billion. They cover 2,289 federal politicians and candidates across 24 election cycles, 1980 to 2026. Those four figures now come from a totals row refreshed once a day rather than from a count over the whole table, and the row we read was written on November 18. The word federal is still the limit on everything this leg can do. Of the 956 state legislators who won this month, 27 have any campaign-finance record at all. Of the 428 US House winners, 425 do.
