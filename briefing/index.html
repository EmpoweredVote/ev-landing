A debt we reported unpaid for three editions

207,185 budgets gained a provenance grade this cycle. The pile of ungraded budgets falls from 245,604 to 38,486. A provenance grade is the sentence that says where a budget figure came from and who, if anyone, checked it. “Unknown” was never a grade on that scale. It meant nobody had recorded the answer, and we had been printing that number unchanged since October 20. The governments holding at least one ungraded budget fall from 3,420 to 1,177. The pile is not 38,419, which is where the grading run left it. A separate load wrote 67 more ungraded budgets a few hours later, and the figure we print is the one after that.

They did not just get a label. All 207,494 rows the run touched gained a source address and a stated basis as well. Of those, 185,507 are graded self-reported and unaudited from a figure we derived, 21,606 are self-reported from a figure the government itself published, and 381 are audited to government accounting standards. The arithmetic closes to the row against the 583,565 budgets we hold. We checked the run had finished before counting any of it, and waited out a second load that started later.

Stance research restarted, after a cycle that added nothing. 43 politicians gained their first researched position. The audit wrote 112 rows and 28 of them were withdrawals, against 459 rows and 252 withdrawals last cycle. Positions a voter is served rose 31,593 to 31,626, the first rise since the audit began. Two map tiles moved, both upward: Alaska from 2 researched officials to 10, and Pennsylvania from 5 to 19.

And we found that we had been counting seven people twice. Eight federal offices carry no chamber in our data: the DC Delegate, its two Shadow Senators, and the Delegates for five territories. The rule that finds officials belonging to no government was shading them onto a state tile, while the coverage table counted them in its national row. They sit in Congress, so the row was right and the tiles were wrong. The table had the opposite fault, and it is the more interesting one. We record a candidacy for the US Senate as a national office, so four sitting state officials who are running were filed as members of Congress. One of them is the Attorney General of Texas. Both faults are fixed, and the numbers they moved are below.

The provenance debt, paid

A provenance grade is the sentence that says where a budget figure came from and who, if anyone, checked it. Ours run from an audited statement prepared under government accounting standards, through a compilation drawn from audited figures, down to a government’s own unaudited filing. That last one is what a small town’s budget usually is, and it is an honest answer. “Unknown” is not a grade on that scale. It means nobody recorded the answer.

Three editions reported this debt unpaid and promised to report the week it was paid. This is that week. 207,185 budgets were graded in a single run on October 28, most of it inside one hour. Every one of the 207,494 rows the run touched also gained a source address and a stated basis, so they are sourced rather than relabelled. 381 of them turned out to be audited to government accounting standards.

What is not fixed is the loader that built the pile. While we were writing this edition it created 67 more ungraded budgets, across three Hawaii counties. That is why the figure we print is 38,486 rather than the 38,419 the grading run left behind. Clearing a backlog is not the same as closing the hole it came out of, and the second job is the one still open.

The purpose, mapped

An interactive Purpose mind map walks the whole vision — the thesis, and the Why, How, Who and What — with a short explainer on every concept and pillar feature.

Sources and method. App statuses come from git history across all repositories, read from each repository’s published branch rather than from a working copy. Every window carries an explicit time of day. This cycle covers the two days since the last edition was committed. One repository, Fallacy Finders, has no remote, so it is the single exception and is read locally. Database counts were queried live on October 29, 2026, against a database with no load in flight. Reach figures are the 30 and 90 days ending October 29, 2026, internal and test traffic excluded, each app counted once across every hostname that serves it.
